# Sport profiles

Use this to translate the 11 research dimensions into sport-specific questions. Qualitative guidance only: pull actual numbers from current sources, never from this file. For a sport not listed, build a profile on the fly with the same headings: *what decides games, what the key metrics are, variance level, availability sensitivity, settlement quirks*.

Contents: Soccer, Basketball, American football, Baseball, Ice hockey, Tennis, MMA and boxing, Cricket, Rugby, Motorsport, Golf, Esports, Universal checks

## Soccer
- **Three-way market**: win/draw/win. Draws are common, so "team X wins" is often much less likely than the price of a big favourite suggests; double chance and draw no bet exist for that reason.
- **Metrics**: goals for/against, expected goals for/against, shots and shots on target, big chances, set-piece threat, clean-sheet and both-teams-to-score rates, over/under hit rates, home/away splits, PPDA/pressing and possession style.
- **Availability**: goalkeeper, centre-backs and the main goal source matter most. Lineups are typically confirmed late; rotation is likely around European midweek games, cup ties, international breaks, and when a team has nothing to play for.
- **Context**: cup ties (two legs, aggregate, away goals rules vary by competition), dead rubbers, derby intensity, red-card swings, weather and pitch quality in lower leagues.
- **Variance**: high; low scoring means upsets and draws are frequent.
- **Settlement**: most markets are 90 minutes plus stoppage time only; extra time and penalties usually excluded unless stated. Check "to qualify" vs 90-minute result. Asian handicap quarter lines split the stake. Abandoned/postponed matches void or stand differently by bookmaker.

## Basketball
- **Metrics**: offensive/defensive rating, pace, net rating, three-point rate, free-throw rate, rebounding, turnover rate, recent minutes and usage, back-to-back and travel schedule.
- **Availability**: star absence moves spreads and totals a lot; load management and "questionable" tags are common; official injury reports and starting lineups appear close to tip-off.
- **Context**: playoff seeding, tanking, blowout garbage time (matters for spreads and player props).
- **Variance**: lower than soccer for moneylines, so strong favourites do win more reliably; spreads and totals are close to coin flips by design.
- **Settlement**: overtime normally counts for moneyline/spread/total; player props can void if the player does not play (rules differ). Quarter and half markets are separate.

## American football
- **Metrics**: EPA per play, success rate, yards per play, pass/rush efficiency, red-zone and third-down rates, turnover margin, sack and pressure rates, pace, special teams.
- **Availability**: quarterback status dominates; offensive line and cornerbacks matter; weekly practice reports and game-day inactives; weather (wind, cold, rain) affects totals and passing.
- **Context**: short weeks, travel and time zones, divisional familiarity, playoff clinching, rest of starters late in the season.
- **Variance**: high per game given few possessions; key numbers (3, 7) matter for spread bets.
- **Settlement**: overtime counts for most markets; pushes (exact spread/total) refund; college and pro rules differ.

## Baseball
- **Metrics**: starting pitcher (ERA is noisy: look at FIP/xFIP, strikeout and walk rates, recent workload), bullpen usage and fatigue, lineup handedness splits, park factors, wRC+/OPS, defence, weather and wind.
- **Availability**: probable pitchers must be confirmed; lineup posted a few hours before; a pitcher scratch can change the bet (many books void or reprice "listed pitcher" bets, check the rules).
- **Variance**: very high per game; even large favourites win well short of their record would imply, so be sceptical of high single-game probabilities and long moneyline accumulators.
- **Settlement**: run line and totals; games shortened by weather and suspended games have special rules; "action" vs "listed pitchers".

## Ice hockey
- **Metrics**: expected goals, shot attempt share, high-danger chances, goalie save percentage above expectation, power-play/penalty-kill, back-to-backs, travel.
- **Availability**: starting goalie confirmation is the biggest single input; check morning skates and team announcements.
- **Variance**: high, low scoring; overtime/shootout decide many games.
- **Settlement**: moneyline usually includes overtime and shootout; "regulation time" (3-way) markets do not; puck line, totals and period markets differ.

## Tennis
- **Metrics**: surface-specific form, serve and return points won, hold/break percentages, ranking vs current form, recent match load and fatigue (long previous matches, late finishes), head-to-head on the same surface, best-of-3 vs best-of-5.
- **Availability and context**: injury or illness doubts, withdrawals and walkovers, altitude, indoor/outdoor, conditions and ball type, motivation at smaller events, tight scheduling around Grand Slams.
- **Variance**: moderate for matches between mismatched players; sets and games handicaps carry extra uncertainty.
- **Settlement**: retirement and walkover rules vary (some void if the match is not completed, or if a set has not been played); check before betting. Set and game totals depend on format.

## MMA and boxing
- **Metrics**: finishing rates and routes, striking and grappling differentials, takedown defence, chin/durability, cardio over rounds, reach/stance, layoff, weight-cut history, recent opposition quality, judging tendencies, venue/altitude.
- **Availability and context**: fight cancellations, replacement opponents on short notice, weigh-in misses, camp changes.
- **Variance**: very high; one clean strike ends it. Method of victory and round markets are even less predictable.
- **Settlement**: draw/no contest rules, "fight to go the distance", round betting; cancelled bouts void.

## Cricket
- **Metrics**: format matters first (Test, ODI, T20); pitch and conditions, toss impact, batting depth, bowling attack by phase, recent form of key players, venue averages, dew and weather forecasts, rain interruption rules (DLS) in limited-overs games.
- **Availability**: playing XI announced at the toss; squad rotation is heavy in franchise and bilateral series.
- **Variance**: high; Tests have a draw outcome and three-way markets.
- **Settlement**: abandoned/no-result games and rain-shortened games void or settle by rules that vary; Super Over rules vary; top-bat/top-bowler markets have their own rules.

## Rugby (union and league)
- **Metrics**: set piece, territory and possession, discipline (penalties), kicking game, bench impact, weather; international windows and rest.
- **Variance**: moderate; favourites win more reliably than soccer; handicaps often decisive.
- **Settlement**: 80 minutes by default; extra time varies by competition.

## Motorsport (F1, IndyCar, NASCAR)
- **Metrics**: qualifying position, race pace, tyre degradation, reliability, track history, weather, safety-car rates, strategy.
- **Variance**: high for race winner markets; reliability and incidents add tail risk. Head-to-head driver markets are settled by classification rules; check what counts for retirements.

## Golf
- **Metrics**: strokes gained by category, course fit, recent form, field strength, weather and tee-time wave (draw bias).
- **Variance**: extremely high for outright winners; top-5/10/20 and matchup markets are less extreme but still wide. Withdrawal rules vary (dead heat, no action, tie rules).

## Esports
- **Metrics**: patch/meta, map pool, roster changes and stand-ins, recent results, online vs LAN performance, best-of format.
- **Caution**: data is thin and shifts quickly; lean on ranges and flag unknowns. Roster changes and patch changes invalidate old form.

## Universal checks (any sport)
- Is the event actually scheduled where and when the ticket says (postponement, venue change, time shift)?
- Are the settlement rules for this bookmaker and market what you assume (overtime, retirement, no-show, weather)?
- Is any critical absence still a coin flip (goalkeeper, quarterback, starting pitcher or goalie, star player, lead fighter, surface specialist)?
- Is this league or competition low-coverage? If so, expect thinner data and wider probability ranges.
- Do the leg's evidence and the market price agree? If not, find out why before trusting either.
